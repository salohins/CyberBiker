# Pistol combat feedback

The player's Shooting Feedback component adds a short camera recoil impulse on each successful pistol shot, a confirmed-hit cross at the projectile contact, and a directional damage/glitch response. Markers follow the target's moving transform and fade after 0.24 seconds. White markers confirm actual health loss; amber marks damaging hits on a finisher-ready enemy. Queued/arriving immunity and the existing pistol stagger rules remain owned by Enemy, so they cannot produce false damage confirmations. The hit-marker canvas is an overlay above scene post effects and never captures input.

Incoming damage adds a brief red directional edge, horizontal glitch bands and restrained RGB separation, plus a camera jolt and thud. The effect fades in 0.38 seconds. It does not modify steering, aim targets or the shared post-processing profile. Camera feedback is removed before the next follow-camera calculation and applied after the existing riding/aim/Launch/finisher channels, avoiding accumulated offsets. The screen component is disabled when idle so it does not add an idle fullscreen copy pass.

Hostile projectiles compare their actual swept segment with the moving player's torso region. A close pass reports only after passing the closest point, at most once per projectile lease, without a shared cooldown: separate bullets in the same burst each give their own cue. An actual player hit suppresses its own near-miss cue. The segment ends at the first obstacle, so a shot blocked before reaching the player cannot cause a through-wall whiz. Friendly shots and distant passes are silent. Near misses play a downloaded bamboo air-swish recording (replacing the tonal whip cue), a faint cyan edge response and four short air streaks. The near-miss level is 0.9. Confirmed hits now use a short recorded body-impact transient at level 0.8 instead of the quiet UI click. Both cues have short edge fades and normalized peaks; no synthetic electrical layer or reverb is added. The tiny mono cues are preloaded as PCM so their transients avoid lossy compression and first-play decoding delays. The audio uses the existing pooled voices, master volume and pause handling. The particle emitter restarts after idle and has a 32-particle cap.

## Enemy body and bullet-contact reactions

A damaging player projectile now passes its swept contact point, surface normal and travel direction to Enemy. A small additive jolt bends the rider's middle spine in the projectile direction, peaks quickly and eases back over a 0.4-second reaction. It is applied after Animator/rig evaluation and removed before the next animation evaluation, so it cannot accumulate or change the enemy root, bike transform, pathfinding, steering or colliders. Disabling the object restores any outstanding offset.

At the same contact point, 40 narrow warm streaks spray outward in a broad cone, in the moving rider frame. Their billboard orientation follows the spray direction projected into the camera, independent of road velocity. The dedicated Impact Spark Streak shader has crisp tapered edges and ordinary alpha blending, with color clamped below HDR levels to avoid an overlapping glowing blob. The particle system is reused for every hit, capped at 192 particles and has no lights or full-screen flash. Pistol hits replace the older center-of-enemy electrical damage prefab with this localized burst. Collision and sword damage keep their existing effects. Queued/arriving immunity does not trigger a body jolt or spark burst. Physical pistol contacts continue to react at the finisher-health floor, while health loss, hit-confirmation markers and the finisher expiry timer retain their original rules.

Tune either enemy prefab's **Enemy Hit Reaction** component: **Jolt Degrees**, **Side Flinch Degrees**, **Torso Kick Distance**, **Recovery Time**, **Sparks Per Hit**, **Spark Width**, **Spark Length** and **Spark Speed**. Both enemy prefab variants are configured; EnemyHitReactionSetup.Configure verifies their existing Enemy settings remain unchanged. ShootingFeedbackChecks.Run covers real projectile damage, a visible bounded spine offset, live contact sparks, immunity, repeated damaging hits entering stagger, unchanged enemy root position and clean recovery. Native close-camera captures are saved as EnemyBefore/EnemyImpact/EnemyRecovered in ShootingFeedbackValidation.

The body reaction uses a 20-degree directional kick, a 10-degree shoulder flinch and a 0.2-unit upper-body dip/shift. Its 0.4-second cycle rises smoothly over 56 ms, holds for 32 ms and returns smoothly over 312 ms. Repeated shots blend from the current recoil instead of snapping back to neutral. Contact streaks are 0.065 units wide and 0.55 units long before per-particle variation, last 0.26–0.365 seconds, travel at 12 units/second relative to the rider and use intensity 1.2. Existing damage, fire rate, enemy movement and finisher settings are unchanged.

The native render check also compares contact sparks enabled/disabled from the normal gameplay camera and requires at least 80 visibly changed pixels, so validation is not limited to close orbit views.

Streak dimensions have a distance-aware minimum of 3.2 pixels of width and 16 pixels of length at a 720p reference before per-particle variation. This keeps the spray readable at normal fighting distance without adding HDR bloom. Spark Width and Spark Length remain the base world-space sizes.

Gameplay visibility: contact sparks render after the finisher highlight (Transparent+30 versus Transparent+10), with ordinary scene depth testing retained. The short spray simulates in the enemy root frame so high-speed weaving does not carry it away from the impact. EnemyHitLiveChecks.Run exercises actual automatic aiming, 70 m/s riding, live traffic, enabled enemy navigation/animation, finisher entry and further pistol contacts at the health floor using the existing aim camera.

Every streak is emitted at the supplied bullet collision point, with no emitter-ring offset. Hierarchy scaling is explicit, with inverse scale applied to particle dimensions/velocity, so the 1.9-scale enemy prefabs keep the spray on the hit surface at the intended world dimensions. The larger 40-streak spray was reviewed from the ordinary moving aim camera, including normal, finisher-entry and health-floor contacts.

The rider's upper-body position and rotation offsets are removed before the next animation evaluation. The separately constrained gun meshes are carried with their hand-target changes while preserving authored offsets, then restored alongside the body. The bike, hips, navigation root, steering, Animator playback speed and weapon balance retain their existing behavior. Live validation measures the actual head movement in screen pixels from the normal moving aim camera, checks both gun/hand offsets and holds the world/camera across fresh rendering frames to compare the skinned body with and without the additive jolt.
