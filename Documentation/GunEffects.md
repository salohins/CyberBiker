# Gun effects

Enemy shots now use the existing Toon Muzzleflash 8 asset, with a dedicated warm material, a short light pulse and a brief smoke burst. The flash happens only when a real projectile is fired. Each gun reuses its own flash object; its first rendered frame is retained even at low frame rates. The effect also reconnects lazily when enemies were already running before the scripts changed.

The default experiment hides both permanent enemy gun lasers. The trajectory is hidden during windup and appears green only when the bullet launches. It stays green regardless of whether the player is in its path. Its committed endpoints use the projectile parent's coordinate frame: BulletLocal follows the player forward, so the guide inherits that same motion and stays aligned with the bullet. It does not retarget or follow the gun after firing. It disappears when the bullet reaches its committed endpoint, hits something, expires or returns to the pool. Guides are pooled, survive shooter destruction while their bullet flies, and hide during pause. Bullet movement, appearance, damage and lifetime are unchanged.

When an in-flight green trajectory's remaining segment crosses the player's enabled solid collision bounds, a compact green danger halo appears around the animated helmet. Twelve tapered, curved strokes ripple outward above and beside the head. The helmet has a separate material with modest HDR brightness, wider/longer strokes and a synchronized pulse; the trajectory line keeps its existing subdued material. Pulse speed increases smoothly from 1.8 to 6.5 cycles per real second as the nearest threatening bullet closes through the last 30 world units. Its phase is accumulated continuously so changing urgency does not snap the animation. Multiple threats use the nearest body-intersection distance, rather than whichever shot was checked first. The effect quickly fades in and smoothly fades out when the rider steers clear, the shot is evaded, or its guide ends. Multiple shots share one warning; a safe shot cannot cancel another dangerous one. It never changes the green trajectory's color or visibility. Pause hides the strokes and freezes their animation/fade. The effect uses cached renderers and an active-flight registry; there are no per-frame scene searches or per-shot effect spawns.


Disable **Enemy Trajectory Guides** to restore the previous laser experiment described below. In that mode each gun has a persistent thin white world-space laser while the enemy is actively aiming. The gun about to fire turns red for the full warning window (0.6 gameplay seconds by default), then returns to white. A 0.2-second recovery separates warnings. The enemy firing cadence expands if necessary so a fast shot interval never cuts the warning short. The enemy-only laser material uses normal alpha blending and bounded non-HDR color instead of additive light. Beam width defaults to 0.03 for the red warning; white is 45% as wide, with a small minimum screen width to remain readable from the chase camera. The shader uses a solid color core with a narrow soft edge rather than dimming the entire thin stroke. Brightness defaults to 0.8. Red warnings sort after idle beams so overlapping white beams cannot wash out their color. The other gun's white beam dims during a red warning so it cannot wash out the warning color. Lasers track the same point as the shot, stop on nearer colliders and hide during pause, arrivals, queued encounters, finishers and death. The former red charge blob and incoming-shot UI remain removed.

The last 25% of the beam near the player fades smoothly to fully transparent at the player/contact point. The first 75% keeps the same color and opacity. A nearer obstacle does not move this fade all the way back to that obstacle. After a real shot, only the firing gun's beam drops to 15% opacity for 0.12 gameplay seconds, then smoothly restores over 0.1 seconds. Pause freezes this response.

Gun smoke reuses Cartoon FX's smoke-fume texture. Particles inherit most of the enemy's forward speed, then are swept backward, spread and fade. Enemy projectiles keep their original mesh, material, scale and tracer, with a separate pale air wake curling behind them. Pooled player projectiles do not retain that wake.

The player's muzzle blast now has a consistent world size independent of the gun hierarchy's scale. It follows the actual shot direction and lasts a short gameplay-time duration, with a minimum of three frames. Existing pooled/deferred returns remain in use. No pistol damage, firing rate, steering, bullet speed or collision shape changes are included.

Select **GameplayManager**, expand **Projectile Combat** under **Enemy shots, muzzle effects and timed evade**:

- Enemy Bullet Trajectory Experiment: Enemy Trajectory Guides, width, brightness, opacity, end fade, line color. Enabled by default; the line appears at actual launch and is hidden before firing.
- Helmet Danger Sense: enable, color, radius, streak length/width/brightness, height, fade in/out, collision-bounds padding, Far Pulse, Near Pulse and Urgency Distance.
- Enemy Shot Warning: Firing Warning (full red window), Enemy Shot Recovery, Enemy Aim Laser, warning width, white width multiplier, brightness, idle color and warning color.
- Enemy Laser Fade and Shot Response: player fade fraction, shot dim duration, restore duration and dim factor.
- Enemy Muzzle Flash and Smoke: scale, brightness, duration, light, smoke count, lifetime, wind sweep.
- Player Pistol Muzzle Flash: scale, brightness, duration, light.
- Bullet Air Wake: enabled, length, width, opacity.

Restart Play mode to load all final assets/settings. Validation uses the isolated Unity Editor project; no Android build is included.

`EnemyTrajectoryChecks.Run` covers the trajectory experiment in the isolated native Editor: hidden windup, exact shot direction, committed flight path, continuous high-speed riding and steering (normal and slow motion), endpoint and collision cleanup, pooled reuse, pause/resume, switching modes, shooter destruction, and a constant green line while moving out of and back into its path. The same check validates helmet attachment, path-triggered warning, fade/re-entry, pause, evasion, registry cleanup and a real moving bullet progressing from the slow pulse to the urgent pulse. Evidence is in `EnemyTrajectoryValidation`.
